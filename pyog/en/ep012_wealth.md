If working hard were enough to make you rich, the people who open the market stalls before dawn and work late into the night in tough conditions would be the richest people in the world.

But they're not.

It's not that they don't work hard enough. They work harder than almost anyone. Which means wealth isn't decided by effort alone.

People who work hard but never become wealthy tend to have something in common. Today, I'll share four things they share.

And I'll show you how what you want actually comes to you. It's probably the opposite of what you think. Along with the story of a piece of paper an unknown actor named Jim Carrey kept in his wallet.

First, you need to know this. In Korean saju, the Four Pillars of Destiny, it's said that everyone has a vessel for wealth. When more money comes in than the vessel can hold, it overflows and drains away.

There's a study of lottery winners in Florida. People who won fifty to a hundred and fifty thousand dollars were, a few years later, going bankrupt at about the same rate as people who'd won very little.

The money came in. But the vessel stayed the same.

Spiritually, your vessel is as big as what you feel you're able to receive. People who work hard but never get ahead have a blocked vessel. So what's blocking it?

The first thing they share. Believing that having less is a virtue. Don't be greedy about money. Money changes people. As long as you're at peace, it doesn't matter if you have little. We've heard these since we were kids.

So we accept going without as normal. We even think having less is humble, or good. And saying what we want starts to feel like greed.

If you want money but also think money is bad, you're calling it with one hand and pushing it away with the other. Having less is not a virtue. It's just one experience. It's okay to want abundance. That's the belief that has to change first.

The second thing they share. Not knowing how to receive. Someone compliments you, and you say, oh no, not really. Someone offers help, and you say, I'm fine. Someone gives you a gift, and you feel guilty. If you only give and never receive, the flow gets blocked. Energy has to come in and go out to keep moving.

The third thing they share. The vibration of lack. I don't have enough. It's never enough. When you want money from that feeling, the feeling of not having just keeps going.

<!-- section: forced | visuals: person staring mirror, sticky notes wall, person sitting alone dark, rain window, tired face | title: Number Four: Forcing Yourself to Believe -->
And the fourth. This is the trap most people fall into. Especially people who search for videos like this one.

I am rich. Money comes easily to me. You say it every day.

But if you're saying it out loud while not believing a word of it inside, it does nothing.

The harder you force yourself to believe, the emptier you feel. Because nothing has actually changed. And eventually you think, see, it doesn't work, and you see the world even more darkly.

The problem was never a lack of belief. The problem was that you were forcing it.

<!-- section: imagine | visuals: woman smiling window light, hollywood hills night, cozy home interior, friends laughing dinner, happy tears | title: This Is How You Get What You Want | ai: a woman sitting by a sunlit window with her eyes closed, a soft smile spreading across her face, warm golden light and tiny floating sparkles gathering around her chest like a glowing heart >> the golden light around her chest slowly brightens and expands, sparkles drift upward, her smile deepens, sunlight shimmers -->
So what do you do instead? Not force yourself to believe, but imagine, joyfully.

When Jim Carrey was still unknown, he'd reportedly drive out alone at night and imagine directors wanting to work with him. It made him feel good. Then he wrote himself a check for ten million dollars. For acting services rendered. He dated it Thanksgiving, a few years in the future, and kept it in his wallet.

And around that Thanksgiving, he found out he'd be paid ten million dollars for a movie.

But there's something he always adds. You can't just visualize, and then go eat a sandwich.

Whatever it is you want, picture yourself already having it. How would you feel? How would you spend your day? Who would you talk to, and about what? If you bought a home, how would you decorate it?

Lightly. Joyfully. Often. So much that you can't help smiling, your chest swells, and you could cry from how good it feels. It's not homework. You do it because it's fun.

And when you're done imagining, you still do what you need to do. Calmly, steadily, you live your real life. That's the difference between someone forcing belief and someone imagining joyfully. One wears themselves out trying. The other moves through life already feeling full.

Of course, you might feel afraid. That feeling is valid too. But whatever you don't want, try not to dwell on it.

A thought becomes an image, and the deeper the feeling you pour into that image, the more likely it is to be created. Ever notice how the thing you worried about seemed to happen faster? That's because we imagine our fears far more vividly.

Feel the fear, but don't give it wings. Give those wings to what you want.

Things come not when you're desperately clutching, but when you're light. Keep that it's-okay-either-way feeling underneath, imagine joyfully, live your real life, and keep practicing.

So starting today, try just three things.

One, five minutes before bed. Breathe deeply until you feel still, then joyfully imagine a day where you already have what you want. If you smile, that's enough.

Two, when someone compliments you, don't deflect. Just say, thank you. It opens your vessel to receive.

Three, whenever money comes in, and whenever it goes out, say thank you. Gratitude is the feeling of already having, so it shifts the vibration of lack faster than anything.
