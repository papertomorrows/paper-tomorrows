First, where does the idea of an Age of Aquarius come from?

The Earth spins like a top. And like a top, its axis slowly wobbles, tracing a huge circle in the sky. Astronomers call this the precession of the equinoxes. Around the second century BC, the Greek astronomer Hipparchus was the first to notice it.

Because of that wobble, the constellation behind the rising Sun on the first day of spring very slowly shifts. One full circle takes about twenty-five thousand eight hundred years. The ancients called it the Great Year, or the Platonic Year.

They divided that great circle into the twelve signs of the zodiac. That makes each age a little over two thousand years long. The last two thousand years or so are called the Age of Pisces. The age now arriving is the Age of Aquarius.

Now, to be honest with you, nobody agrees on exactly when it starts. Some say it has already begun. Others say it is still centuries away. And this isn't proven science. It's a symbol, a way people have used the sky to make sense of their times.

But symbols have power. The stories we live by shape the lives we live. So today, let's not worry about the date. Let's listen to the message inside the symbol.

Astrologers say each age leaves its symbol on history. From roughly four thousand to two thousand years ago came the Age of Aries, and rams appear again and again in its myths and rituals. Then came the Age of Pisces, and one of its defining symbols was the fish. Early Christians even used a simple fish sign to recognise each other. In 1951, the psychologist Carl Jung explored the symbolism of this Piscean age in depth in his book Aion.

The Age of Pisces is often described as an age of belief. Great authorities held the answers, and people followed them. Truth lived somewhere outside of us, and someone else delivered it.

Spiritually, it was an age of looking outward for salvation. Waiting for someone to save us. Waiting for someone to tell us the answer.

The Age of Aquarius turns that around. Truth is no longer handed down from above. It awakens from within. We are moving from following the teacher outside, to meeting the teacher within.

That doesn't mean teachers and teachings no longer matter. It means that instead of simply obeying, we hold every teaching up to our own heart, and trust what truly resonates there.

And here's something remarkable. On December twenty-first, twenty twenty, Jupiter and Saturn met extraordinarily close together in the night sky. And where did it happen? At the very start of Aquarius. For many people, it felt like a door opening.

So the change of this age isn't just a revolution that tears everything down. It's the work of building a new freedom, responsibly. Awakening and maturity, walking side by side.

That 2020 meeting becomes even more meaningful here. Jupiter and Saturn meet roughly every twenty years. Astrologers note that from 2020, for about two hundred years, these meetings will mostly happen in air signs. They call this shift from an earth era to an air era a Great Mutation.

If the earth era was about possessions and material things, the air era is about ideas and information, relationships and consciousness. Which sounds a lot like the world we're living in right now.
