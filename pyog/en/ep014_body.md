Meditation. You want to try it, but when you actually do, it doesn't really work, does it?

You close your eyes and sit, and somehow there are even more thoughts. Tomorrow's to-do list, something someone said, an old memory out of nowhere. And eventually you give up, thinking, maybe meditation just isn't for me.

If you're new to meditation, that's completely normal. It's not that you're bad at it. You just did things in the wrong order. Sitting still and stopping your thoughts from the very start is hard even for people who've practiced for years.

So at first, don't sit. Move first.

But it's not just about moving. It's about the few seconds right after you stop moving. A tiny gap. How you use that gap is what today is really about.

Awakening doesn't come through the head. It comes through the body. Today, I'll talk about how to use your body to quiet your thoughts, and how to plant what you want in the gap when they stop. And at the end, we'll get up and do it together.

Thoughts are always off in the past or the future. But your body is always here, now.

And emotions stay in the body. Psychiatrist Bessel van der Kolk, in his book The Body Keeps the Score, describes how wounds that words couldn't reach often begin to loosen only when the body starts to move.

That's why heavy energy only clings tighter when you try to think it away. You have to shake it out, through the body.

On Earth, your body is your soul's instrument, and its antenna. When the instrument is stiff, it's hard to hear your soul.

That's why, for thousands of years, people met God through dance.

Sufis dress in white and spin, one palm turned to the sky, one to the earth. They turn, and turn, until the self disappears, and it's said they become one with God.

Korean shamans opened the door to other worlds with drums and dance. And Native Americans cleansed body and mind with sweat, in the heat of the sweat lodge.

And all of these have one thing in common. They stop thought.

Milton Erickson, often called the father of modern hypnotherapy, would deliberately keep talking in confusing, monotonous ways. As the conscious mind tried to follow and wore itself out, at some point thinking would let go. And in that gap, people slipped into deep trance.

Zen koans work the same way. What is this? You hold on to a question the mind can never solve, again and again, until thinking reaches a dead end, and simply snaps. That moment becomes the door to awakening.

The body works the same way. Dance until you're out of breath, move until the sweat runs, and for just a moment, thinking stops completely.

Thought is like a gatekeeper. When it's awake, it blocks the way: that'll never work. But in the moment the gatekeeper dozes off, that's your chance to let what you want slip through.

So why do we meditate, really?

For most people, it's because they want to be happy while they're here in this world. Healthy, abundant, at peace.

Of course, some people meditate because they want to expand their consciousness so far that they won't need to come back to Earth. That's a beautiful path too.

But today, I'll talk about the kind of meditation most people want. Meditation for living better, here, in this world. Quieting the mind, and in that stillness, picturing the life you want.

In that gap, picture what you want as one vivid scene.

Not a goal, not a sentence, but like a scene from a movie. Where you are, who you're with, what you see, what you hear.

And load that scene with big emotion. Overflowing joy, gratitude, happiness so strong it brings tears.

In an earlier video, I said to imagine joyfully. This gap is when that imagining sinks in the deepest. While the gatekeeper rests, that scene goes in, unblocked.

And of course, you can also just rest in that stillness without picturing anything at all. The longer you stay there, the more you feel connected to something deeper in the universe. That's a wonderful time too.

If you're new to meditation, try just these three things, starting today. Sitting meditation can wait until this feels familiar.

One, one song a day. When no one's watching, dance however you want. You don't have to be good at it.

Two, right after you finish dancing or exercising, while you're still out of breath, close your eyes and picture one scene you want.

Three, on heavy days, before you start thinking, shake out your whole body for three minutes.

Go at your own pace, within what your body can handle. Even gentle shaking is enough.
