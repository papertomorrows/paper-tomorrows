# EP007 (EN) "If You Want It Badly Enough, It Will Happen" Is Only Half True | Why Letting Go Makes Things Happen

Have you ever noticed that the more desperately you want something, the further away it seems to go?

The message you kept waiting for that never came. The exam you clung to and still failed. The plan you gritted your teeth over, that only got more tangled.

And maybe you've had the opposite experience too. The moment you finally said, fine, whatever happens, happens. And then, almost like magic, everything fell into place.

That's not a coincidence.

The saying "if you want it badly enough, it will happen" is only half true. There's a missing half. And that missing half is the power of letting go.

Today, we'll explore why things work out better when you let go, and how to dream vividly and take action while releasing your grip on the outcome, through spiritual wisdom and science. And at the end, we'll do a three-minute practice together: picturing your wish, and then letting it go.

<!-- section: paradox | visuals: woman anxious phone, man stressed office, clock ticking, rain window, tangled rope | title: The Paradox of Wanting -->
First, why does wanting something desperately push it away?

There are two kinds of wanting. One comes from excitement. The other comes from fear.

Wanting that comes from fear quietly says: without this, I'm not okay. I'm not enough as I am.

So we grip the outcome tightly. We check on it again and again. We get impatient and anxious. But the tighter we grip, the more our body tenses, our mind narrows, and our judgment clouds over.

Like squeezing a handful of sand. The tighter you squeeze, the faster it slips through your fingers. Open your hand, and the sand stays.

<!-- section: science | visuals: athlete stadium, basketball player, students exam, scientist lab, runner sunrise | title: What Science Says About Letting Go -->
This isn't only a spiritual idea. Science points the same way.

Psychologists study something called choking under pressure. The cognitive scientist Sian Beilock showed that when people try too hard to perform well, they often make more mistakes. Even movements you've practiced a thousand times become clumsy the moment you obsess over the result.

The psychologist Daniel Wegner ran a famous experiment. He asked people not to think about a white bear. And the white bear kept popping into their minds even more. The harder we try to force the mind, the more it pushes back.

And the positive psychologist Barbara Fredrickson found that when we feel relaxed and positive, our field of view widens. When you're anxious, you only see the problem in front of you. When you're at ease, you start noticing opportunities and new paths all around you.

That's why the world's best athletes so often say the same thing. They performed their best not when they were trying to win, but when they were simply enjoying it and fully immersed.

<!-- section: spirit | visuals: ancient temple, river flowing, monks meditation, lotus, mountain mist | title: What the Spiritual Traditions Teach | ai: an ancient archer in flowing robes standing on a misty mountain at dawn, drawing a glowing bow, the golden arrow about to be released toward a distant star >> the archer slowly draws the glowing bow and releases, the golden arrow flies toward the star leaving a trail of light, mist swirls -->
The spiritual traditions have known this for thousands of years.

In the Bhagavad Gita, there's this teaching: you have a right to your actions, but never to the fruits of your actions. So do your work, without clinging to the result.

That doesn't mean doing things half-heartedly. It means acting with your whole heart, and then trusting the result to a larger flow.

The Taoist sage Lao Tzu spoke of wu wei. Not doing nothing, but not forcing. Flowing like water. And the softest thing in the world, water, eventually wears through the hardest stone.

And Buddhism teaches that the root of suffering is clinging. Wanting isn't the problem. Gripping is.

Picture an archer. The archer looks at the target and draws the bow with all their strength. But for the arrow to fly, at the very end, they have to let go. Hold on to the arrow, and it will never reach the target.

<!-- section: three | visuals: woman journal morning, man working laptop calm, hands planting seed, walking nature, sunrise field | title: The Three-Part Balance of People Whose Dreams Come True | ai: a person kneeling in a softly glowing garden at dawn, gently planting a small seed that sends a thin thread of golden light up toward the sky >> the golden thread of light rises slowly from the seed into the sky, tiny sparks drift, morning light spreads over the garden -->
So how do people whose dreams come true actually live? They hold three things in balance.

First, they dream vividly. Not a vague "I hope it works out," but a clear picture of the moment it's already happened. What they're wearing, who they're with, how it feels.

Second, they act. They don't just dream and wait. They take one step they can take today, even a small one, again and again.

And third, they let go. Once they've done their part, they don't cling to the result. They know when and how it happens is out of their hands. So they live today lightly and at ease.

Think of a farmer who plants a seed. The farmer plants it, waters it, makes sure it gets sunlight. But the farmer doesn't dig it up every day to check if it's sprouted. If they did, it would never grow.

Dreams are the same. Plant them, tend them, and then trust and wait.

<!-- section: why | visuals: woman smiling city, handshake meeting, couple happy, friends laughing, man confident walking | title: Why Wishes Come True When You Let Go -->
So why do wishes come true when you let go?

First, you start to see. When you're gripping, you only see the one path you decided on. But when your mind relaxes, unexpected opportunities, people, and better paths come into view.

Second, you can go the distance. People who cling to results burn out and give up. But people who enjoy the process keep walking. And most dreams come true for the ones who keep going.

Third, people are drawn to you. People are drawn to someone calm and at ease far more than someone anxious and desperate. In love, in work, in negotiation. Desperation pushes away. Ease pulls in.

And fourth, the most spiritual reason of all. The moment you let go, you're no longer struggling alone. You begin working with something larger than yourself. Some call it the universe, some call it God. You do your part, and you trust the rest. And that's when you reach your dreams in ways you could never have imagined on your own.

<!-- section: heaven | visuals: ocean waves sunset, night sky stars, sunrise clouds, waterfall, milky way, lotus | title: When Where You Are Becomes Heaven | ai: a person sitting peacefully in meditation where the sky is split between a golden sunrise on one side and a deep starry night on the other, both halves blending into one radiant circle of light around them >> the golden day and the starry night slowly swirl together into one glowing circle around the meditating figure, stars and sunlight mingle, soft light pulses -->
But there's an even deeper layer to letting go.

Many spiritual teachers say that heaven isn't a place you go after you die. It's a state of being you can enter right here, right now.

What kind of state? A state where you accept the person in front of you, and whatever is happening to you, exactly as it is, without pushing it away. The good and the hard alike.

How is that possible? Because, in the end, everything is one.

The Earth we live on is a material world. A world of duality. Light and darkness, good and bad, right and wrong all seem separate here.

But the place this material world is born from, the deepest source of the universe, is different. It's where everything simply is. Pure energy, before anything was divided.

Picture the ocean. Waves rise high, and fall deep. But the high wave and the low wave are both the same ocean.

From a deep, high, and wide enough view, light and darkness aren't separate. Because of the darkness, the stars shine. Because of winter, spring blooms. Light and darkness are both beautiful, and both precious. Even the most painful chapters of your life.

When you accept everything like this, the place where you are becomes heaven. And the closer you come to this state, the faster your wishes come true.

Why? Because you're no longer resisting. A mind that pushes things away separates you from the whole. But a mind that accepts everything reconnects you with it. And when you're one with the whole, your wish is no longer just your wish. It becomes a wish the whole universe is helping to fulfill.

Of course, accepting doesn't mean enduring what harms you. It means, instead of hating and resisting, first seeing things as they are. And then choosing wisely. Leaving what you need to leave, in peace. Protecting what you need to protect, in peace.

<!-- section: remember | visuals: starry sky person silhouette, cinema screen light, mountain summit sunrise, woman meditation lake, light rays forest | title: The More You Remember, the More You Create -->
And all of this leads to one question. Who am I? Who are we?

We're not only this body, this name, or the roles we play right now. We're a consciousness that existed long before all of that. A single light that came from the whole. Many spiritual traditions say we simply forgot this when we were born. So the spiritual path isn't so much about learning something new. It's about remembering who you've always been.

The better you know who you are, the more deeply you remember, the more something remarkable happens. In any situation, you can take a step back and see it from the seat of the observer.

Even when something makes you angry, you know there's the part of you swept up in the anger, and the part of you quietly watching it. Like being the main character in a movie, laughing and crying, while at the same time sitting outside the screen, watching the film.

From the observer's seat, you can choose from somewhere other than fear. Not from other people's opinions, not from anxiety, but from what brings your soul the most joy.

And as you follow those choices, one by one, at some point you realize that the wish you longed for has been coming true on that very path.

A wish coming true isn't a gift that falls out of the sky. It's a creation, made by the one who remembered who they are, walking the path of their soul's joy. You are the creator of your life.

<!-- section: notgiveup | visuals: woman thinking window, mountain climber, sunrise road, river rocks, steady walking | title: Letting Go Is Not Giving Up -->
But there's something important to be clear about.

Letting go is not giving up.

Giving up means dropping the dream and the action. Letting go means keeping the dream and the action, and releasing only your grip on the outcome.

People who give up stop. People who let go keep walking, lighter. From the outside they may look alike, but they're heading in completely different directions.

<!-- section: signs | visuals: man checking phone, woman anxious, clock night, crowded city, tired face | title: Five Signs You're Gripping Too Tight -->
So are you gripping too tightly? Check these five signs.

One. You check on the result several times a day.

Two. When someone else gets there first, you feel anxious before you feel happy for them.

Three. You often think, if this doesn't work, it's over.

Four. You try hard to change other people's feelings or situations to fit what you want.

Five. When you think about what you want, you feel urgency before excitement.

How many fit you? Leave the number in the comments. And it's okay if it's a lot. The moment you notice, letting go has already begun.

And this connects to the age we're living in now.

The Age of Pisces was an age of struggle. We believed rewards only came through blood, sweat and endurance.

But the Age of Aquarius is an age of flow. The people who move ahead aren't the ones forcing things, but the ones flowing as themselves. Not the ones trying to control everything alone, but the ones who trust a larger flow and create together with it.

The water bearer doesn't squeeze the water out. The water bearer simply tilts the vessel. And the water flows on its own.

<!-- section: method | visuals: morning journal, sunrise window, walking nature, cup of tea window, calm night bedroom | title: Dream, Act, Let Go: Three Steps -->
So starting today, try this.

Step one: one minute each morning, picture it vividly. Close your eyes and imagine the moment your wish has already come true. Then feel the joy and gratitude of that moment in your body.

Step two: take one step you can take today. However small. One message. One page. One new thing learned.

Step three: let it go. Before you sleep, breathe out slowly and say: I did my part today. I'll trust the rest.

And one more thing. Don't score the result. Score the process. If you took one step toward your dream today, today was already a success.

<!-- section: together | visuals: candle light, calm lake, stars night sky, light rays clouds, sunrise ocean | title: Let's Try It Together -->
So let's take three minutes together, right now.

Sit comfortably and close your eyes. Breathe in slowly… and breathe out, long.

Now, bring to mind the one wish you most want to come true.

That wish has already come true. Where are you right now? Who's with you? What's the look on your face?

Feel that joy filling your chest. And silently say, thank you.

Now, rest your hands on your knees, palms open to the sky.

Take a deep breath in… and as you breathe out, long and slow, let the wish go. As if releasing it into the sky.

I'll do my part. I'll trust the rest.

And now, accept this moment exactly as it is. Just as it is, it's okay. Right here is heaven.

Slowly, open your eyes. Do you feel lighter? That lightness is the path your wish travels on.
