First, we need to separate two things. Loneliness and solitude are not the same.

The theologian Paul Tillich put it like this. Loneliness is the word for the pain of being alone. Solitude is the word for the glory of being alone.

You can be just as alone, but loneliness is a state of having lost yourself. You wait for someone to fill you. Solitude is a state of having come back to yourself. No one is there, and yet you feel full.

The aloneness that feels comfortable to you right now is probably solitude.

So why does a season like this come? There are four reasons.

First, because you've outgrown your shell. As a lobster grows, its hard shell starts to squeeze, and it hurts. So it hides under a rock and sheds the shell. Then it waits, alone, until the new one hardens. The discomfort you feel now is a sign that you've grown.

Second, because your energy has changed. When your frequency shifts, you no longer resonate with the old people, the old conversations, the old fun. No one did anything wrong. It just doesn't fit anymore.

Third, because you have to empty before you can be filled. A cup full of other people's expectations and the world's noise has no room for anything new. Time alone is time to empty that cup.

Fourth, because your luck is changing seasons. In Korean saju, the Four Pillars of Destiny, it's said that this happens often when a great cycle of fortune turns, when new luck is opening. People fall away, you want to be alone, and the old ways stop fitting. Just as your body feels off when the seasons change, your heart and relationships shake when your fortune changes. Spiritual traditions call this the time right before a great awakening.

Of course, right now it may feel awkward, uncomfortable, even frightening. You don't have to force yourself to be happy about it. That feeling is valid, just as it is.

But you're not falling apart. These are growing pains, from a process of growing in every part of your life and rising to a higher level.

Pascal once wrote: all of humanity's problems stem from man's inability to sit quietly in a room alone.

Maybe that's why great awakenings have always been preceded by solitude. Forty days in the wilderness. The Buddha sitting alone beneath the Bodhi tree. Countless meditators in mountain caves.

In game terms, this is a save point. A place to stop and rest before the next stage. Time to log out of someone else's game, just for a while.

And now, the most important thing about this season.

You, whoever you are, never came to Earth to take care of someone else. You never came to please anyone, or to satisfy anyone.

We came to keep discovering what our soul wants. To remember ourselves, and to remember us. To have a body, and among all the experiences only Earth can offer, to choose and live more of the ones our soul wants.

So in this season, be more selfish.

Selfish doesn't mean hurting others. It means filling yourself first. On a plane, they tell you to put on your own oxygen mask first. You have to breathe before you can help the person next to you. An empty pitcher can't pour for anyone.

Speak, think, and act by how your soul feels. Say what your heart wants to say, not what others want to hear. Instead of asking how others will see you, ask first: how does my soul feel right now? Choose what makes your soul lighter, over what you're supposed to do.

That doesn't mean caring for and loving others is wrong. Raising a child, caring for someone who's sick, a job that looks to everyone else like enormous sacrifice and service, that might be exactly the experience your soul wants.

If so, do it, no matter what anyone says.

What matters isn't what you do, but why you do it. What you do to satisfy others, to be a good person, empties you. But what you do because your soul wants it, however much it looks like sacrifice, fills you.

And accept everything. The time alone, the people leaving, the discomfort right now. In the end, we are all that is. Everything simply exists, and everything is energy. In truth, right and wrong don't exist. They're just names we gave things. So there's no reason to fit yourself to anyone else's standard.

<!-- section: seven | visuals: morning journal, cozy bed morning, decluttering room, walking nature, bookshop browsing, calm night bedroom | title: Seven Things to Do in This Season -->
So what should you do in this season? Just remember seven things.

One, be selfish without guilt. When you turn down plans, you can say: today, I have plans with myself.

Two, take care of your body first. Sleep enough, walk, eat something warm. When your soul is changing, your body needs rest too.

Three, empty out. Tidy your room, let go of clothes you don't wear, and unfollow the accounts that make you uneasy every time you see them. New energy comes in when there's space.

Four, stop comparing. This isn't the time to watch other people's pace. Inside the cocoon, you don't need to look at anyone else's wings.

Five, follow small pulls. A book, a place, a song you're drawn to for no reason, that's your soul giving directions.

Six, write. Before bed, just one line. What did I truly want today? Who am I?

Seven, don't rush big decisions. Quitting, breaking up, moving, make those choices in stillness, not on impulse. In this season, the answers become clear slowly.
