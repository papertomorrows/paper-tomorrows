# EP009 (EN) Why People Who Think About Death Every Day Live the Most Freely | Death Saved My Life

Death saved my life.

Sounds strange, doesn't it? This starts with a story from a novel. But maybe, it's your story too.

What if someone told you, today: you have exactly one week left.

What would you do tomorrow?

In Paulo Coelho's novel Veronika Decides to Die, Veronika is twenty-four. She has a job. She's young, beautiful. But every day feels the same, and empty.

Exhausted by life, she wakes up in a psychiatric hospital. And a doctor tells her: your heart is damaged. You only have days left.

And from that very moment, Veronika truly starts to live, for the first time.

And there's a secret the doctor kept in this story. I'll tell you at the end of this video.

Let me say this first. This isn't a story that encourages death. It's the opposite. It's about how remembering death is what finally lets us live. And if you're going through something really hard right now, I've put places you can reach out to in the description.

<!-- section: veronika | visuals: piano playing hands, woman laughing, woman walking snow, moonlit window, candle light | title: What She Did When Time Ran Short -->
Once Veronika believed she had only days left, she stopped caring what anyone thought.

She played the piano in the middle of the night, a childhood dream she had put away. When she was angry, she showed it. When she loved something, she said so. And for the first time in her life, she truly fell in love.

With nothing left to lose, her fear was gone.

This story is asking us something. Do we really need death right in front of us before we start to live?

<!-- section: mementomori | visuals: ancient rome statue, sunset hourglass, old library books, stars night sky, candle light | title: Memento Mori: Remember You Will Die | ai: an hourglass glowing with golden sand on a wooden table by an open window at night, the starry sky outside, a single candle beside it >> the golden sand slowly falls through the hourglass, the candle flame flickers, stars twinkle through the open window -->
In ancient Rome, it's said that when a victorious general paraded through the streets, someone behind him would whisper: memento mori. Remember that you, too, will die.

Why remind someone of death at their most glorious moment?

Steve Jobs gave the answer. Remembering he'd be dead soon was his most important tool for big choices. In the face of death, expectations, pride, and fear of failure fall away. Only what matters is left.

Research even shows that people who imagine their own death become more grateful for life. Things they took for granted start to come back into view.

<!-- section: beauty | visuals: cherry blossoms falling, sunset ocean, fireworks night, autumn leaves, child laughing | title: Because It Ends, It's Beautiful | ai: cherry blossom petals glowing softly as they fall through warm evening light over a quiet river, each petal shimmering like a tiny lantern before it touches the water >> glowing petals drift slowly down and touch the river, ripples of golden light spread, the evening light deepens -->
That's why the old wisdom says: think of death every day. In Bhutan, a country known for its happiness, there's even said to be a saying: think about death five times a day.

Because death is what gives life its value.

Picture cherry blossoms. If they bloomed all year long, we wouldn't feel that thrill beneath them. It's because we know they'll fall in a few days that this moment is so beautiful.

Think of a morning on the train to work, staring blankly at your phone. If that had been the last morning of your life, would you have spent it that way?

If we lived forever, today would mean nothing. But our days are numbered. So today is a single day that will never come again.

Death isn't the enemy of life. As precious and beautiful as life is, death is just as precious and beautiful, a part of life. So there's no need to fear it, and no need to rush toward it. It comes naturally, in its own time, as life's completion.

<!-- section: loss | visuals: mother child hug, old couple holding hands, autumn leaves falling, candle light, hands holding | title: Love More, Because You Will Lose It | ai: two hands gently holding a glowing golden flower in the dark, some of its petals slowly drifting away into the air as soft light, the flower still shining warmly >> a few glowing petals slowly drift away from the flower into the dark air, the flower keeps shining warmly, the hands hold it gently -->
Everything we love will one day leave us. The people we love, our health, our youth, this very moment.

That doesn't mean don't love. It doesn't mean keep a cold distance so you won't get hurt. It's the opposite.

In one of his sonnets, Shakespeare wrote to a lover watching him grow old: This thou perceiv'st, which makes thy love more strong, to love that well which thou must leave ere long.

Knowing you'll lose it soon, no, because you know, you love it even more. You hug them today. You say thank you today. You say sorry today.

Gaining, being together, is wonderful. But losing is another experience of the very same value. If there were only gaining, we'd never know how precious being together is.

But little by little, you step out of the fear of losing. Not love that clings tight out of fear, but love held in an open palm. That's the deepest love, and the freest.

But here's what's truly remarkable. Losing isn't really an ending.

<!-- section: energy | visuals: ocean waves, milky way, light rays clouds, stars night sky, aurora | title: We Are Energy That Never Disappears | ai: a glowing ocean wave made of golden light rising from a vast sea of light under a starry sky, then gently returning and dissolving back into the shining sea >> the golden wave slowly rises and curls, then gently melts back into the glowing sea, ripples of light spread outward, stars shimmer -->
Because we're not just a body that lives this one life.

Science tells us energy doesn't disappear. It only changes form. Spiritual traditions say the same of us. The body is clothing we wear for a while. The real us is energy, consciousness, that never disappears.

The deeper teachings go further. Time is just a frame we use to experience this world. At the source, everything exists at once. The past, the present, the future. The people who've left, and the people beside us now.

Picture the ocean. A wave rises, then returns to the sea. The wave didn't disappear. It simply went back to the ocean it always was.

So losing isn't really an ending. It's a change of form. Even when someone we love leaves, the energy of that love doesn't disappear. It's still here, in us, and in this universe.

Someone who knows this doesn't fear losing. And so they can love more freely, and more deeply.

<!-- section: freedom | visuals: woman dancing field, open road sunrise, bird flying sky, person arms open mountain, ocean waves | title: When Fear Is Gone, You Live as Yourself -->
And when you no longer fear death, or losing, what changes?

Other people's opinions get smaller. On a stage that will one day disappear, there's no reason to cling to anyone's judgment.

Failure stops being scary. The only thing you can truly lose in this game is never having lived as yourself.

You start what you've been putting off. Not someday. Today. Just like Veronika playing the piano in the middle of the night.

And above all, you live each day more as yourself, and more freely. Someone who knows their time is limited has no time to live someone else's life.

<!-- section: practice | visuals: morning journal, sunrise window, hugging family, woman dancing field, calm night bedroom | title: Live Today Like the Last, but Lightly -->
So starting today, try just three things.

First, once each morning, think of death. Then ask: if today were my last day, would I want to do what I'm about to do?

Second, tell the people who matter, today. Thank you. I love you. I'm sorry. Don't put it off.

Third, do one thing a day like Veronika. One small thing you've held back because of other people's eyes. Do it today.

<!-- section: together | visuals: candle light, calm lake, stars night sky, light rays clouds, sunrise ocean | title: Let's Try It Together -->
So let's take one minute together, right now.

Sit comfortably and close your eyes. Breathe in slowly… and breathe out, long.

Imagine that today is the last day of your life.

Now look back at the life you've walked so far. The days you laughed and the days you fell. All of them are days you lived.

Now, take a deep breath in… and as you slowly breathe out, open your eyes.
