# EP017 (EN) Uniqueness Is My Power

Ever since you were little, did you feel a bit different from everyone else?

A comment everyone else laughed off stayed with you for days. After being around a lot of people, you felt drained for no reason. And the path everyone said was good somehow never pulled at you.

So you shrank back. Am I strange? Am I too sensitive?

Today, I want to answer that feeling. You're not strange. Inside that difference, there's a power that's only yours.

And I'll tell you the story of two people who changed the world with the very part of themselves others told them to fix.

No two souls are the same.

Each of us came here carrying the experiences we most want to have in this life, and the things we want to create on this Earth. And we were born remembering, and holding on to, what we'd need for that.

The traits that made you feel uncomfortable because you were different. The parts of you that you wanted to hide. Those aren't broken pieces.

They're your gifts, connected to what your soul came to focus on and experience more intensely in this life, and ultimately, to create here on Earth.

Especially if you've often blamed yourself for being too sensitive, please hear this.

After years of research, psychologist Elaine Aron found that about one in five people is born with a temperament that feels far more finely, and processes far more deeply, than others. Not a flaw. A natural trait.

People like this notice small changes others miss. They read what isn't said, they're moved more deeply by beauty, and they know how to stay beside someone who's hurting.

That's why they tire easily, too. Not because they're weak, but because they're taking in that much.

<!-- section: breath | visuals: woman painting canvas, hands arranging flowers, woman laughing friends, potter hands clay, woman writing journal cafe | title: What Comes as Easily as Breathing | ai: a woman effortlessly weaving glowing golden threads into a beautiful tapestry with calm, easy hands, while people around her watch in quiet amazement >> her hands move easily as the glowing tapestry grows more beautiful, the people around her lean in, golden light spreads -->
And there's this, too. Something others find hard, or don't even know how to do, that comes to you as easily as breathing.

Putting a stranger at ease in minutes. Seeing at a glance how to organize a mess. Knowing instantly whether colors and spaces work together. Sensing what someone feels without a word.

These come so easily that you don't even realize they're gifts. Doesn't everyone do this, you think, and move on.

No. Not everyone does. Your soul packed that on purpose.

<!-- section: proof | visuals: woman reading library, quiet woman writing notebook, woman alone bookstore, cattle farm sunrise, woman speaking stage light | title: When What Made Them Different Became Their Power | ai: a quiet woman sitting alone in a warm library at night, writing in a notebook, a soft golden light glowing from her pages and drifting out into the dark city beyond the window >> golden light rises gently from her pages and drifts out the window over the city, lighting small windows one by one -->
Susan Cain was a quiet, introverted child. People told her to be more outgoing, and she believed she had to be.

So she acted like an extrovert, and worked as a Wall Street lawyer for seven years.

Then she turned toward the very quietness she'd tried so hard to fix, and explored it deeply. The book she wrote, Quiet, brought comfort to introverts all over the world, and her talk has been watched by tens of millions of people.

Animal scientist Temple Grandin is autistic. As a child, she was late to speak, and being different made life very hard.

But she thought in pictures, not words. So she could see what frightened animals before anyone else could. With that difference, she designed facilities where animals suffer less, and changed the way countless farms work.

The part of them that was different was exactly their power.

No one asks a rose to grow tall like a sunflower. No one asks a sunflower to smell like a rose. Each one simply blooms as itself.

Compared to others, you'll always look a little strange. It was another flower's ruler to begin with.

The only one who can measure you is your own soul.

<!-- section: find | visuals: woman journaling night, child playing alone happy, woman looking old photo, woman thinking window, morning light desk | title: Four Questions to Find What's Uniquely Yours | ai: a woman sitting by a window at dawn holding an old childhood photo, a soft glowing memory of her as a little girl happily absorbed in drawing appearing beside her >> the glowing memory of the little girl drawing brightens beside her, the woman smiles softly, dawn light grows -->
So how do you find your own gift? Ask yourself these four questions.

One. When did I shrink back, thinking, am I strange? On the other side of that discomfort, a gift is often hiding.

Two. What do others struggle with, that comes unusually easily to me?

Three. What do people keep asking me for, or asking me about?

Four. As a child, what did I lose track of time doing?

Where the answers overlap, that's where your gift lives.

<!-- section: practice | visuals: morning journal writing, woman calm window, woman hand on heart, person walking slowly park, calm night bedroom | title: Three Things to Start Today -->
So starting today, try just three things.

One, when you catch yourself thinking, am I strange, say this instead. This is a sign of my gift.

Two, pick just one of the four questions today, and write down your answer.

Three, once a day, instead of hiding what makes you different, let a little more of it show.
